decision: D-pending · the "copper shine on simpler." interior rule counts half-covered taper pixels as letter pixels, so it fails in whichever engine rasterises a taper at 35% to 47% coverage. Proposed smallest change (not made): count a pixel as interior only if its eight neighbours at 1 px are also glyph (instead of four at 2 px), or compare only pixels whose own coverage is at least 90%.

Why not the outline: the first attempt (`--stage-accent-stroke: .012em`) failed the "copper shine" cases in Chromium. Cause: a stroke widens the ink into narrow gaps and joins, so more half-covered pixels pass the test's interior rule (a pixel and its four neighbours 2 px away all differ from the hidden-letters shot by more than 150). The colour fringe in CI (orange on Light, blue on Evening, same spot x=115 y=26) is the same thing seen through CI's subpixel text rendering: a half-covered pixel. The stroke colour is currentColor, so the test's `color: transparent` hides it correctly; nothing copper leaks. Locally (greyscale text) the stroked word fails at other pixels for the same reason; the pixel set depends on the rasteriser.

Measured in Chromium with the test's exact rule at 388-392 and 1436-1444 px wide, Light and Evening, at device scale 1 and 2.625 (32 cases per variant): staging as is (opsz auto) 0 failures, worst 6.8:1; stroke .012em 12 failures; opsz 44: 0 failures, worst 6.8:1; opsz 28 and 30-32: 12 failures. No stroke or weight change was safe in all cases. The worst WebKit pixel (about 35% to 47% coverage) sits at a taper next to the "r" and the full stop, so thickening cannot be shown to lift it to the roughly 60% coverage 3:1 needs; that is a property of the test's interior rule, not of the word.
